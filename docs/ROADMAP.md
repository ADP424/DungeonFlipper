# Dungeon Flipper — Feature Roadmap

## Context
The game has a working core: you clear a room to own it, then renovate and furnish it. The owner's notes describe a big expansion:
- property as a living economy (tenants, rival realtors, utilities, staff)
- securing property under constant monster pressure
- infinite, difficulty-graded floors
- a Backrooms-style comedic retheme

This roadmap records the design decisions made so far (D1–D54), what they mean for the engine, and the phases they ship in. Each phase gets a detailed plan in `docs/plans/` when it starts. The open questions listed under each phase are answered then.

### What exists today (from the code survey)
- **Ownership** is `RoomState.owned: boolean`, set the moment a room is cleared (`roomOwned`). Doors have no state (`DoorInfo {x,y,dir,toRoom}`). Enemies can't cross door tiles, and combat is client-only, per room.
- **Enemies** are persisted only as `deadSpawnIds` (template coordinates). There is no threat or tier field (10 enemies, 1 boss), and difficulty is only an HP multiplier based on floor depth.
- **Floors** are finite, with 10–14 rooms generated by `FloorBuilder` (`world/generator.ts`). `FloorBuilder` can resume from an existing floor, so floors can grow later.
- **Off-screen time:** `world/advance.ts` (`registerResolver`, `advance`) exists but nothing calls it, and `globals` is unused.
- **Furniture** drops through the loot tables (`loot/roll.ts`, `enforceSingleTileFiller`). Map furniture (`*`, `placeMapFurniture`) exists.
- **Persistence:** a pure `applyCommand` reducer that the server replays; schema v2 (`save/schema.ts`).

## Decisions

### Time and economy
- **D1 (revised) The phone clock works like Stardew Valley.**
  - The phone shows the time whenever you look at it (hover or click).
  - ~~Each room transition adds 15 minutes.~~ Superseded by **D53**: the clock runs in real time.
  - **The day ends when you sleep.** You can sleep anywhere *secured*, right where you stand, and you wake **8 hours** after you lie down, but never before **6 AM** the next morning.
  - **At 12 AM:** if you're in a secured area, you're forced to sleep on the spot. If you're in an unsecured area, you have until **2 AM** (2 more in-game hours) to reach secured ground. Otherwise the screen fades to black and you die (D15 rules).
  - **Sleeping outside the apartment bed** is allowed, but you wake at **2/3 HP**. The apartment bed is where you heal. Getting home is quick because the estate is seamless. Later: more fast travel (e.g. a buildable monorail; out of scope).
  - **Sleeping resolves the day tick:** rent in and out, decay, incursion rolls, utility faults, tenant and staff jobs, realtor moves.
  - The clock lives in `globals` (`day`, `minutes`).
  - Resolvers in `advance()` use seeded RNG over `(worldSeed, day, key)` and emit ordinary commands, so the server stays a pure replayer.
- **D51 Only the apartment bed is a bed you sleep in.**
  - It is fixed: it can't be lifted.
  - Every other bed is ordinary furniture.
  - Sleeping anywhere else secure happens only through the phone, or when you're forced to at midnight.
  - Furniture gets generic `fixed` and `use` fields (click behaviours), which the lobby elevator and utility sources will reuse.
- **D7 Rent is paid automatically on the day tick** and reported in the morning digest. Haggling and complaints happen in person.
- **D8 Unpaid apartment rent becomes debt with escalating pressure:** landlord notices, then a comedic repo event that takes some stash or furniture. It is never a game over, and early rent is cheap.
- **D15 Death costs only the unstashed inventory**, as today. Property, tenants and gold remain.
- **D20 Reputation is one global number with tiers.** Tiers unlock wealthier tenant pools. Evictions, NPC deaths and neglected complaints lower it.

### World
- **D5 Floors are infinite.**
  - They grow lazily outward from the lobby.
  - Saves are split into region docs so that `floor:N` doesn't grow without bound.
  - Going downstairs starts a harder baseline and a new biome/"Level".
- **D29 Difficulty = floor baseline + cell distance from the lobby**, so it forms rings. It is deterministic under lazy generation.
- **D33 Spawns use generic slots plus a threat budget.**
  - Template characters mark slot kinds.
  - A seeded pick fills each slot from enemies whose `threat` fits the room's difficulty.
  - `EnemyDef` gains `threat` and `tags`.
- **D34 Boss rooms are rare and random.**
  - Each boss has a minimum difficulty, so farther out means harder bosses.
  - A floor can have several boss rooms.
  - "Boss room has stairs" is a soft template rule, not an engine invariant.
- **D17/D23/D30 Lobbies, apartment and travel:**
  - Each floor's entry room is a **lobby** with an elevator. NPCs arrive there.
  - Your **apartment** is a tiny real-world room whose portal opens into floor 1's lobby. Its bed is the only place you fully heal by sleeping (D1).
  - Elevators fast-travel between lobbies you've discovered.
- **D28 Room authoring keeps the ASCII maps** and adds optional parallel `layers` (same dimensions, own legend) and a `placements` list for rotated or multi-tile things, utility sources, elevators and terminals. Player-built things are always `RoomState` diffs, never template data.
- **D11 Furniture comes from the world, not from drops.**
  - Enemy furniture drops are removed. Pre-placed furniture in a *cleared* room can be hauled home, and better furniture appears farther out.
  - Other sources: realtor trade and depot orders.
  - Carrying furniture into unowned rooms stays blocked.

### Securing
- **D13 (revised in P3) Claiming uses a company flag; securing uses barricades.**
  - A cleared room is claimed by planting a company flag. You start with 4, and more come as loot; crafting them from found materials comes later.
  - Security only needs barricades on doors into rooms that still have monsters.
  - Unbarricaded doors into such rooms let monsters in often (25% per 15-minute tick).
  - The original wording follows: **Securing uses barricades only.**
  - A room is secured when it is cleared **and** every frontier door (a door facing a non-secured room) has a barricade of at least the tier the neighbour requires. The required tier is f(the neighbour's difficulty).
  - A cleared room with no frontier doors becomes secured automatically.
- **D9 Barricades work as doors.** Higher tiers are lockable doors that you pass through freely and monsters must bash, and that bashing is the decay. Low-tier planks have to be removed and re-placed.
- **D37 (revised in P3) A barricade belongs to the doorway.** It's mirrored onto both door tiles with one shared HP, so it shows, blocks and can be removed from either side. You can't barricade between two rooms you own. The original wording follows: **A barricade is stored on the secured side**, in that room's `RoomState`, keyed by door. If both sides become secured, the barricade becomes optional.
- **D10 (revised in P3) Monster rosters and incursions:** every unbarricaded door that doesn't lead to calm owned property spills monsters into visited rooms each hour, until the room's threat capacity. A cleared room refills unless it's barricaded, and it's only owned once every door is barricaded or leads to your rooms. The original wording follows:
  - Rooms persist a roster (def id plus HP) of *all* monsters present, which replaces the semantics of `deadSpawnIds`.
  - Template spawns never respawn.
  - On day ticks, unowned frontier rooms roll incursions into their rosters (capped by difficulty) and put pressure on adjacent barricades. Nothing unseen is simulated.
- **D3 (revised) Breaches:** when a barricade reaches 0 HP, monsters enter. You have **until the end of the day after the breach** (two sleeps) to re-secure the room before you lose ownership.
- **D14 (revised) Rescue window:**
  - Tenants in a breached unit flee to adjacent secured space if they can.
  - If they can't, they're **killed at the end of that same day** (your next sleep) unless you clear the room first.
  - This is deliberately very punishing, because there's a lot of prep you can do to prevent it.
- **D45 (superseded by D53) Only screen transitions cost time** (one-screen dungeon room ↔ dungeon room, and estate ↔ dungeon). Moving within the seamless estate is free, so building, haggling and hauling never eat the day.
- **D46 No breach cascade.** Neighbours of a breached room A stay secured. A becomes a "hot" incursion source like an unowned room: its monsters push on the neighbours' openings every hour. Barricaded openings decay; unbarricaded ones let monsters spill through, which breaches that neighbour. Prep means pre-barricading internal openings.
- **D47 Player-built walls have HP, much less than barricades.**
  - They work as a desperation layer, not a sealing strategy. There's no arbitrary "can't wall a doorway" rule.
  - Monsters bashing walls off-screen is resolved abstractly on hourly ticks, like barricades.
  - Barricade/wall pressure becomes a generic "frontier segment HP" model: each passable or blocked boundary tile gets a material, an HP and a decay rate.
- **D48 (revised in P3) Everything off-screen runs on 15-minute ticks or once per day**, with chances scaled to match. The original wording follows: **Decay and incursions resolve every clock hour** (every 4 transitions). Sleep runs 8 hourly ticks in a row, so breaches can happen mid-day. That gives the rescue deadline real teeth.
- **D49 Original dungeon walls stay structural.**
  - Monsters can never break them, even once they're internal.
  - They stay **visually distinct** from player-built walls. If wallpaper is allowed on them, some cue must survive, such as trim or thickness. That needs art.
  - The player can demolish one only with a **heavier tool** plus **holding left-click for 1–2 s** to confirm, since it can't be undone. This keeps the WASD+mouse rule.
  - The tutorial is out of scope for now.
- **D50 A built wall on a frontier opening counts as tier 0.** It blocks monsters while its HP lasts but never satisfies the securing requirement.
- **D43 Internal walls and true boundaries:**
  - Where secured rooms connect, dungeon boundary walls become **internal walls**, but they keep their structural strength and look (D49).
  - Room boundaries stay in the data. A phone toggle shows the true room boundaries as an overlay.
  - **If a room gets overrun**, every passable tile on its old boundary (a demolished stretch of wall) becomes an extra frontier opening. Each one needs a barricade and is an extra incursion source, so the spawn rate rises.
  - So knocking down walls has a real security cost.

### Property and engine
- **D2/D6/D16 The seamless estate:**
  - **All secured rooms**, whoever owns them, form one seamless streamed space.
  - Unsecured dungeon rooms stay one-screen combat rooms with door transitions.
  - Ownership becomes a per-room `ownerId`.
- **D53 (after P5) The clock runs in real time, like Stardew Valley:** 1 game minute per real second while you play (a 6 AM–midnight day is 18 real minutes). It stops while a menu or panel is open, while asleep, and in the Escape pause menu (which also holds settings and, later, the main menu). Doors cost no time. Off-screen ticks still fire every 15 game minutes, and the room you're in plus the one you just came from neither send nor receive monsters.
- **D54 (after P5) No arbitrary preventions.** The player can do anything their character physically could, anywhere: renovate, build, demolish, knock through structural walls, carry and place furniture, and plant flags, in rooms they don't own and rooms with monsters. Rooms joined by a knocked-through wall are one area whoever owns them. Bad ideas are discouraged by consequences (rival realtors, later), not by rules. This supersedes the "renovation only in owned rooms" rule and the old block on carrying furniture into rooms with monsters (D11). The portal fires on contact, like stairs, and carries furniture. The Hardware app also sells furniture, delivered onto the apartment floor.
- **D52 (P4) The estate is every connected room you own**, secure or not (via doors or knocked-through walls). The original "all secured rooms" wording in D2/D6/D16 is superseded.
- **D38 The seamless estate supports full combat.** This covers breaches, rescues and thugs. Monsters can chase you across internal doors.
- **D12 Units are auto-detected enclosures plus signs.**
  - An enclosed region bounded by walls and doors that has a For Rent or For Sale sign is a unit.
  - An unsigned region touching several units is shared space.
  - A narrow connector is a hallway.
- **D24 A unit is rentable only if it connects to its floor's lobby through secured rooms.** If a breach cuts that path, tenants get upset and may leave.

### NPCs
- **D44 Tenant tiers:**
  - Every tenant has a tier, and each tier has a hard rent ceiling. Their valuation = min(tier ceiling, how much their preferences value the unit).
  - Tier 1 tenants (the early game) cap out low no matter how nice the unit is.
  - Reputation tiers (D20) unlock higher tenant tiers, and it's those higher ceilings that make luxury renovation pay off.
- **D18 Haggling is an offer plus a mood meter.** Each NPC has a hidden maximum valuation that comes from its preferences and is capped by its tier (D44). You get a couple of counteroffers, and pushing too hard hurts their mood: shorter tenure and more complaints.
- **D32 Tenant preferences are partially revealed:** 1–2 headline likes and dislikes, with the rest found through talking and observing.
- **D39 Unit capacity comes from furniture:** single beds, double beds and separate bedrooms (sub-enclosures). Families need multiple bedrooms.
- **D26 Tenants and staff run live when visible and abstractly off-screen.**
- **D27/D36 Realtors** show up when you sell to them (cooperative) or by squatting on vacant secured units (competitive or adversarial).
  - Personalities toward you are cooperative, competitive (undercuts your rents) or adversarial (steals, sends thugs).
  - Theft makes them adversarial, desperation makes them competitive, and concessions shift them back down.
  - Their rooms are ordinary rooms with an `ownerId` and real furniture in them, and you trade with them in person. Details are designed in their phase.

### Utilities
- **D19/D21 Utilities are physical networks:**
  - Pipes and wires form a hidden layer inside walls and floors, stored as a `RoomState.utilities` diff.
  - The layer never blocks anything and is shown as an x-ray overlay only while you hold a utility tool.
  - Faults show up as symptoms (puddles, sparks) that you trace back to the bad segment.
- **P6 as decided:** electric fixtures (lamps included) need power, but only in property someone owns (rooms nobody owns run on the Backrooms' own hum; the apartment is on the landlord's supply). Networks have no capacity limit. Utilities raise every tenant's valuation, and some tenants require one. Faults are random per tick, shown by puddles or sparks, and repaired with the laying tool. Details in `docs/plans/P6-utilities.md`.
- **D22/D25 Sources:**
  - Every lobby has a **generator** (power) and a **water source**.
  - More sources appear in generated rooms farther out, and you must secure them and connect them up.
  - Each utility has its own source type.

### Theme and UI
- **D4 Commit to the Backrooms theme now.**
  - You play an ambitious developer exploiting an infinite space.
  - The horror is light and comedic.
  - The intro starts in a shabby apartment.
- **D35 Items are improvised, dual-use tools** from apartments and offices, with twists. **D40** Durability is spent **only on renovation**; attacking is free. Real weapons such as guns come only as late-game real-world purchases.
- **D41 An in-world smartphone holds the UI:**
  - It's a tiny buzzing icon by the hotbar, driven by mouse.
  - Its apps are the Messages digest ("while you were out…", by severity), Listings, Depot shop and Map.
  - This keeps the minimal-GUI rule.
- **D42 The multiplayer vision is undecided.** Everything stays **actor-agnostic**:
  - Realtors use the same command surface and `ownerId` semantics as players.
  - Commands keep their `actorId`.
  - How days sync across players stays an open problem.

### Revised design rules (CLAUDE.md to update during P0/P1)
- "Enemies never respawn" becomes: *template spawns* never respawn; incursions add monsters along the unsecured frontier.
- "Living enemies reset to template spawns" becomes: rooms persist their monster roster.
- "Enemies drop at most one tile-filling item" is removed, since furniture comes from the world, trade and depots.
- "No build mode" still holds: barricades, pipes and signs are all placed by the player character using items.

## Engine implications (the key technical risks)
1. **Streaming estate (P4) is the largest rework.** These are all built per room today:
   - `Game.loadRoom`
   - the camera clamp
   - `RoomLayout`
   - the lighting and shadow textures (`render/Lighting.ts`, `render/Shadows.ts`)
   - the BFS flow fields in `RoomRuntime.ts`

   The plan:
   - Each cell (19×11) is a runtime chunk. The working set is the visible chunks plus a margin, with an LRU of warm chunks. Tiles are drawn in per-chunk cached containers.
   - Light and shadow maps are **camera-sized**, and lights and occluders are culled to the visible range plus the light radius.
   - `RoomLayout` is split into per-chunk derived grids that keep the derivation order (cells → template → doors → overrides → furniture).
   - Pathing becomes hierarchical: a per-chunk nav grid plus a chunk/door graph.
   - Entities leaving the active set freeze into their room's roster.
   - Keep the hard-won rule that per-room lists are cleared together with their views, applied per chunk.
2. **Determinism:** every off-screen outcome is a seeded resolver that emits commands, and the reducer stays clock-free.
3. **Schema v3+:**
   - `ownerId`
   - rosters
   - door barricades
   - `utilities`
   - region docs
   - clock globals
   - migrations from v2 saves (owned → ownerId = player; `deadSpawnIds` → rosters).
4. **Infinite generation:** lazy frontier generation through `FloorBuilder.attach`, seeded per region, with special-room placement (bosses, sources, depots) governed by distance and density rules.
5. **Notification fatigue:** batch everything into the digest and push only urgent items (breaches with trapped tenants).

## Phases (each one ships playable; each gets its own detailed plan)
- **P0 Foundations:**
  - `ownerId`
  - the phone clock (15 min per screen transition, hourly resolvers)
  - sleeping anywhere secured, forced sleep at 12 AM and 2 AM death, waking at 2/3 HP away from the apartment bed
  - a dev key to skip time
  - monster rosters
  - `threat`/`tags` on enemies
  - `layers`/`placements` in templates
  - phone shell with digest
  - schema v3 with migration
- **P1 Infinite world:**
  - lazy generation and region docs
  - cell-distance difficulty
  - slot and threat-budget spawns
  - rare boss rooms with fairness rules
  - lobby and elevator
  - furniture drops removed; carryable world furniture instead
- **P2 Theme pass** (parallel from P1):
  - apartment intro and portal
  - retheme of existing content
  - dual-use tools
  - asset audit
- **P3 Securing:**
  - the frontier segment HP model (barricades, built walls as tier 0, structural dungeon walls)
  - frontier and auto-secure logic
  - hourly decay and incursions
  - breaches with deadlines
  - breached rooms as hot sources
- **P4 Seamless estate:**
  - chunk streaming
  - camera-sized lighting
  - hierarchical pathing
  - combat in the estate
  - enclosure detection
  - *Likely split:* P4a rendering/streaming, P4b simulation/pathing
- **P5 Tenants v1:**
  - singles
  - signs and listings
  - arrival through the lobby
  - preferences, haggling and rent
  - lobby-connectivity rule
  - rescue window
- **P6 Utilities:** sources, x-ray pipe and wire layer, fixtures, faults and symptoms, repair
- **P7 Tenants v2:**
  - couples and families (capacity from beds)
  - mess and cleaning
  - staff hiring
  - eviction
  - NPC death and reputation tiers
- **P8 Rival realtors:** trade, squatting, personalities, competition, theft and thugs, concessions
- **P9 Depots:** rare far-out rooms, phone ordering, pickup
- **P10 Late game:** real-world purchases (guns, etc.)

## Open questions, deferred to each phase's plan
- **P1:**
  - What's the fairness floor for finding a way down? For example, the boss chance per ring rises until one is guaranteed.
  - How many difficulty rings per threat tier?
  - How far ahead of the player should generation run?
- **P3:**
  - Barricade tier costs and materials, and decay rates per threat.
  - Grace length.
  - Incursion caps.
  - Can monsters on an incursion pass through a breached room into a second room?
- **P4:**
  - What are the performance targets (max estate size, chunk margin, light count)?
  - What does the screen transition look like between the estate and one-screen rooms?
- **P5/P7:**
  - The preference catalogue.
  - Mess generation rate.
  - Eviction flow.
  - How rent and reputation numbers scale.
  - What happens to a tenant's belongings when they leave.
- **P6:** pipe/wire capacity and range, whether faults happen on day ticks or in real time while you're present, and fixture list per utility.
- **P8:** realtor economy, thug encounters, theft mechanics, concession costs.
- **P0:** answered in [plans/P0-foundations.md](plans/P0-foundations.md).
- **Global:**
  - Tune the day length: 6 AM→12 AM is 72 transitions.
  - How does the economy balance (own rent vs. income vs. depot prices)?
  - How do days sync in multiplayer?
